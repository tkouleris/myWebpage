Post Title

Published on: Sept 22, 1982

Five years ago, in November, I decided to leave Linux, which had been my companion since 2005, and go back to Windows.

I made that decision after getting tired of waiting all those years for “the year of the Linux desktop” to finally arrive, while there was still no consistent approach to user-friendly UI design or hardware support. I was tired of wanting to buy a peripheral and having to research whether it was compatible with Linux, instead of simply focusing on whether it did the job I wanted it to do.

But now I'm getting tired of big corporations and the direction they seem to be heading in: the idea that **you own nothing**.

So, a few days ago, I insteadlled the latest Ubuntu 26.04. As it turns out, choosing this particular distribution was a big mistake.

A lot has changed in the Linux world since I left it five years ago. Snaps are probably the worst thing to happen to Linux distributions, and Canonical has decided that they are the only way forward, supposedly for the sake of user security.

I don't remember having to deal with Snaps five years ago, but now they're everywhere in Ubuntu. You install an application and, unless you know what you're doing, it can't communicate with your peripherals or access your drives. You have to run commands just to give an application access to removable media and joysticks.

At first, the idea sounds good. An application running in its own isolated environment, without interfering with the rest of the system. But in practice, you sacrifice usability. You increase disk space and memory consumption, and the operating system gradually stops being what it was advertised as for so many years: **YOUR operating system, one that you can make your own and do whatever you want with.**

What's more, Canonical plans to move even more components to Snaps in the next LTS release. EVERYTHING! Even the operating system's kernel.

I have a feeling it won't be long before I leave Ubuntu for another distribution. Probably something Arch-based.
